Gambia

The Gambia is the smallest country on mainland Africa and is well known for the Gambia River, which has supported trade, farming, fishing, and transportation for centuries.

The country has natural resources such as fertile agricultural land, fish, silica sand, and small deposits of minerals, including titanium-bearing minerals and zircon. Agriculture, especially the production of groundnuts (peanuts), has long been an important part of the economy. The Gambia is also known for its rich wildlife, national parks, and tourism, which contribute to the country's development.
