Nigeria

Nigeria is one of Africa's most resource-rich countries. Important discoveries include crude oil and natural gas, first discovered in Oloibiri in 1956, as well as tin, coal, gold, iron ore, limestone, and other valuable minerals. Nigeria is also known for the ancient Nok Civilization, famous for its terracotta sculptures, and for powerful kingdoms such as the Benin Kingdom, Oyo Empire, and Kanem-Bornu Empire. Before oil became the country's main source of income, Nigeria was a leading producer of cocoa, palm oil, groundnuts, rubber, and cotton.

Nigeria has a rich and diverse culture, with more than 250 ethnic groups, each having its own traditions and customs. The country is known for colorful festivals, traditional clothing such as Agbada and Ankara, and lively music and dance performed during celebrations. Respect for elders and strong family values are important parts of Nigerian society. Traditional marriage ceremonies, storytelling, and hospitality are also highly valued. Popular Nigerian foods include jollof rice, pounded yam, egusi soup, suya, and fufu. Christianity, Islam, and traditional African religions all play an important role in the daily lives and cultural practices of the Nigerian people.
